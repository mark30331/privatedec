## Personal Statement

I seek a National Interest Waiver because I intend to continue building privacy-preserving software infrastructure that supports healthcare research, safer data sharing, and responsible AI development in the United States.

My proposed endeavor is centered on the development of `Privatedec`, a software platform that helps institutions prepare sensitive structured datasets for lower-risk analysis, research collaboration, and AI-related use. The platform is designed to reduce privacy risk while preserving practical usefulness through a combination of technical and governance controls, including anonymization workflows, secure storage, signed delivery, retention policies, and reproducible reporting.

I chose this direction because I believe one of the major challenges in modern healthcare and research is not only collecting data, but using it responsibly. Valuable datasets often remain underused because organizations face legitimate concerns about privacy, re-identification risk, compliance burdens, and unsafe sharing practices. At the same time, the United States increasingly depends on high-quality data infrastructure to support healthcare innovation, advanced analytics, and responsible AI systems. I want my work to help address that gap by building systems that make safer data use more practical and repeatable.

My background has prepared me well for this endeavor. I earned a Bachelor of Science in Software Engineering with high academic distinction and I am completing a Master of Science in Information Systems at the University of Utah with a 4.0 GPA. My education has given me a strong foundation in software systems, databases, and information management, while also strengthening my ability to think about technical systems in terms of real organizational needs.

Professionally, I worked as a Software Engineer at Capgemini, where I built backend services and data-processing workflows using Python, Flask, Docker, AWS, and related tools. I worked on systems involving API development, real-time data ingestion, deployment automation, and cloud-based infrastructure. That experience taught me how to build reliable, production-oriented systems that reduce operational friction and support real users. I also gained valuable security and quality assurance experience in a prior QA engineering role, where I helped automate API testing and identified a critical vulnerability that could have exposed user data. These experiences are directly relevant to my current endeavor because privacy-preserving data systems must be both technically sound and operationally trustworthy.

In addition to industry work, I have served in teaching and tutoring roles in databases, Python, Java, and mathematics. Those roles strengthened my ability to explain technical concepts, support others in learning complex material, and think clearly about how data systems are designed and used. I believe this matters because my long-term goal is not only to build software, but to contribute to the broader adoption of responsible data practices.

My current work on `Privatedec` reflects these combined strengths. I have already built a functioning platform with an end-to-end workflow for CSV upload, dataset preview, schema detection, quasi-identifier and sensitive attribute selection, asynchronous processing, secure file storage, signed result delivery, retention controls, and evidence-style report generation. I also incorporated healthcare-focused legal, security, retention, and AI governance pages because I believe real-world privacy-preserving systems must include policy-aware design, not just technical mechanisms.

I believe this endeavor has substantial merit and national importance. The ability to prepare sensitive data for safer research use is important to healthcare institutions, universities, public-interest research, and responsible AI development. The United States benefits when institutions can use data more effectively while reducing privacy risk and improving governance. My work contributes to that goal by building practical infrastructure rather than offering only abstract concepts.

I am also well positioned to advance this endeavor. My academic preparation, hands-on engineering experience, cloud and backend expertise, security awareness, and track record of building complete systems all support my ability to continue developing this platform and related privacy-preserving technologies. I intend to keep strengthening the project through better reporting, auditability, retention enforcement, case studies, and collaboration with researchers and domain experts.

Finally, I believe it would benefit the United States to allow me to continue this work without restricting it to a single employer through the labor certification process. The public value of privacy-preserving healthcare data infrastructure extends beyond one job or company. It can support multiple institutions, research groups, and future collaborations across the country. My endeavor is therefore broader than a position with any one employer, and I intend to continue advancing it in ways that contribute to U.S. research capacity, safer data use, and responsible innovation.

For these reasons, I respectfully submit that my proposed endeavor merits favorable consideration under the National Interest Waiver framework.
